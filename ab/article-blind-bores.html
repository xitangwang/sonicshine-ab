What blind bores change in a cleaning process

Plan liquid access, air release, drainage and inspection before treating an internal feature as cleanable.

Quick answer

Plan liquid access, air release, drainage and inspection before treating an internal feature as cleanable.

Map the internal geometry

Start with a drawing that shows blind ends, intersecting passages, plugs and assembled features. Two parts with the same external dimensions can have very different internal access. Mark the surfaces that matter to the next operation and identify which can be inspected directly. A photograph of the outside is useful context but does not describe the full cleaning task.

Give liquid and air a route

A submerged part is not automatically filled throughout. Orientation, closed ends and nested loading can leave air where the process needs liquid access. Review how the part enters the bath and whether the intended fixture supports a useful orientation. Do not assume extra ultrasonic power compensates for a feature the liquid cannot reach.

Follow what remains after cleaning

The same recess that holds machining fluid may retain cleaning liquid or rinse water. Consider drainage during transfer and the condition required before drying or assembly. A short external drip does not establish that an internal cavity has drained. Include the full cleaning and finishing route in the assessment rather than judging the bath stage alone.

Choose an inspection method in advance

Agree how the relevant internal surface will be assessed. The method depends on the geometry and customer requirement; accessible visual checks alone may leave important areas unverified. Record the method, sampling positions and acceptance owner before a trial. Keep the difference between “not observed” and “verified clean” explicit in the report.

Use representative trial conditions

Trial the actual material and residue with a realistic fixture and load pattern. Record conditions and note which features remain uncertain. Results from one open passage do not establish performance in all blind bores. If a design or orientation changes, compare it against the agreed inspection method before treating the new arrangement as suitable for production.
