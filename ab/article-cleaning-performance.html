Cleaning performance has changed: what should you check first?

Compare the process record before changing the recipe or assuming the ultrasonic system has failed.

Published / updated 2026-11-09Editorial guidance3 min read

Quick answer

Compare the process record before changing the recipe or assuming the ultrasonic system has failed.

Describe the change precisely

Record what changed, when it appeared and which parts are affected. Distinguish visible residue, retained liquid, surface damage and failed inspection. Keep an example of the incoming condition as well as the outgoing result. A useful troubleshooting request includes the machine identifier, active stage and current settings, not only “it is not cleaning.”

Compare load and incoming residue

Check whether material, part geometry, soil type, residue age or basket density has changed. A different machining fluid or longer storage can make the incoming problem different. Look for altered orientation, nesting or blocked access. If the production load differs from the trial load, restore a representative comparison rather than treating the original result as a permanent guarantee.

Check permitted operating conditions

Using the model’s instructions, review liquid level, bath condition, temperature and time settings. Check whether filtration or bath maintenance is due. Confirm the full route, including rinsing and drying, because contamination may be carried forward or reintroduced after cleaning. Keep electrical and safety-system checks within qualified service procedures.

Change one thing at a time

Record a baseline, then change only a justified process variable within the approved operating range. Use the same inspection method and comparable samples. Changing chemistry, loading and time together can conceal the actual cause. Do not bypass protective devices or open electrical enclosures as an experiment. If equipment faults are suspected, stop and contact qualified support.

Make the support request actionable

Send the before/after process record, material and residue details, basket photographs, alarms and maintenance history. State what has already been checked and whether the issue repeats. The next step may be a process review, a representative trial or model-specific service. A general article cannot diagnose a particular electrical, mechanical or ultrasonic fault remotely.
