Why tank capacity is not usable basket space

Compare part geometry, basket clearance and working liquid level before using litres as a selection shortcut.

Quick answer

Compare part geometry, basket clearance and working liquid level before using litres as a selection shortcut.

Measure the complete loaded envelope

Start with the largest realistic combination of part, fixture and basket. A tall part may need a different orientation to drain correctly, and that orientation changes the required space. Add room for handles, dividers, lid movement and safe loading. A drawing of the loaded basket is usually more useful than a single nominal part dimension.

Separate three different dimensions

Tank dimensions, basket dimensions and the actual working envelope are different quantities. A basket sits above the tank floor and may leave clearance at its sides. The useful liquid level can be below the tank rim. Ask the supplier to show the usable area on the current drawing rather than converting catalogue litres into an assumed part size.

Check weight and access

A load can fit geometrically while exceeding a basket or handling limit. State the total weight of parts, basket and fixture, including any retained liquid relevant to handling. Dense packing can restrict access and draining. Increasing the bath volume alone does not resolve those problems. Confirm how the complete basket enters, leaves and travels to the next station.

Review capacity conventions

A published capacity may use a nominal or geometric convention rather than the operating fill with your load in place. Ask what the value means, what level is required, and how displacement and overflow allowance are handled. Confirm the definition before comparing two catalogues. Do not use capacity alone to predict throughput.

Make a useful sizing request

Send the limiting part drawing, preferred orientation, loaded basket sketch, total weight and the next operation. Mark any dimension still open. Request a current layout and a written basket/load specification for the proposed equipment. This turns a broad capacity enquiry into a review of whether the actual work can be handled.
