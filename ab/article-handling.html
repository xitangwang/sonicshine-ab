Why basket loading belongs in the cleaning process design

Part orientation, spacing, loaded weight and drainage deserve the same attention as the cleaning equipment. Document the loading pattern before scaling a process.

Published / updated 2026-11-09Editorial guidance3 min read

Quick answer

A cleaning cycle is applied to a loaded basket, not to an isolated drawing of a part. A repeatable loading arrangement makes a process easier to assess, explain and reproduce.

1. Define the unit of production

Decide what one load means. Record the part count, part weight, basket weight and any dedicated fixtures. Identify whether a load contains identical parts or a mixed batch. A mixed batch can introduce differences in geometry and handling that are easy to miss in a simple capacity estimate.

Photograph the proposed arrangement from above and from the side. Mark the orientation of components with internal passages or delicate surfaces. These records help a supplier understand the intended operation and give operators a clear reference during later assessments.

2. Leave room for the process

BANDELIN’s operating guidance calls for suitable baskets and warns against placing parts directly on the tank bottom. Its loading guidance also addresses spacing and trapped air. These are useful general principles to check against the instructions for the equipment actually being used.

For a specific application, review how parts sit in the fixture and whether a recess can fill and drain. A blind cavity facing the wrong way may retain air or liquid. Discuss sensitive contact points and how the load can be supported without damage. Do not assume that the maximum possible part count is a suitable process load.

3. Design for transfer and drainage

Follow the basket from loading to unloading. Check lift access, the transfer path and the space needed at each station. The basket should fit the handling arrangement, and the proposed lifting method should be reviewed for the fully loaded condition.

Include drain time and the path of carried liquid in the process discussion. A basket can move fluid from one stage into another, so its orientation and drainage deserve attention. Make any containment, drip area or transfer requirement visible on the layout instead of leaving it to be solved after installation.

4. Put the loading pattern in the record

If the production load changes, treat the new arrangement as a change to be reviewed. An evaluation with a few loosely placed parts does not automatically establish the behavior of a densely filled production basket.

5. Compare complete cycles

When discussing throughput, count loading, transfers, draining, unloading and any inspection activity. Describe whether those tasks happen sequentially or can overlap. This gives the equipment team a clearer picture than a cleaning time alone.

A well-documented basket arrangement connects part protection, process access and handling. Bring the proposed load to the equipment discussion early; it is much easier to revise the loading plan before the tank and transfer arrangement are fixed.
