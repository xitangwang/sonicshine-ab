From cleaning enquiry to equipment proposal

A practical guide to the information, decisions and documents that connect an initial enquiry with a clearly defined Sonic Shine equipment discussion.

Published / updated 2026-11-09Editorial guidance3 min read

Quick answer

A useful enquiry does not have to contain every engineering answer. It needs enough information to identify the part, explain the cleaning problem and make the next technical discussion specific.

1. Establish the starting point

Begin with part photographs, material, dimensions and the reason cleaning is required. Explain the current method and the problem that prompted the enquiry. A rejected coating, visible machining residue and a difficult maintenance task are different starting points, even when the parts fit in the same tank.

Include the production location, preferred contact channel and approximate project timing. These details help frame the conversation, but they do not replace the technical information. Keep commercially sensitive drawings separate until the appropriate sharing arrangements have been agreed.

2. Create a shared requirement record

Turn the conversation into a short written brief. Record the incoming condition, desired outgoing condition, representative load and available floor space. Identify who will confirm the acceptance method and which part variations the assessment must cover.

A useful record distinguishes a customer requirement from a proposed solution. For example, “the parts must be dry before packaging” describes an output; a particular drying arrangement is a solution to be evaluated. Preserving that distinction keeps alternatives open during the technical review.

3. Connect the process to a layout

Discuss the equipment family only after the workflow is clear enough to explain it. A single-tank arrangement, a multi-stage process and a handling system each organize the work differently. Review where loading, transfers, draining and unloading take place.

Draw the working area around the equipment as well as the machine footprint. Operators need access, baskets need a transfer path, and service points must remain reachable. A layout should make those activities visible rather than showing only a row of tanks.

4. Make the proposal scope easy to read

Ask for important assumptions to appear alongside the relevant equipment or service item. This makes the proposal easier to review internally and reduces confusion when several people are involved in purchasing.

Proposal areaQuestion to resolve
EquipmentWhich model family, tank arrangement and controls are included?
Part handlingWhich baskets, fixtures, loading and transfer functions are included?
UtilitiesWhat site connections and installation work are required?
AssessmentWhat samples, conditions and acceptance checks are agreed?
DocumentationWhich drawings, instructions and records are supplied?
Commercial scopeWhich options, support activities and exclusions are listed?

5. Confirm the next decision

Before moving ahead, summarize what has been agreed and what remains open. A sample evaluation may still be needed; a site connection may require confirmation; a basket arrangement may need revision. Assign an owner to each open item.

The goal is a proposal that the customer and equipment team can discuss using the same terms. Contact Sonic Shine with the part information you already have, and use the first conversation to identify the information needed for the next step.
